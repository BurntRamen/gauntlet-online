# Campaign perspective and expansion guide

## Scene starters

When a chapter changes viewpoint, open with a compact scene card that answers three questions before the dialogue begins:

- **Where:** the planet, settlement, vessel, or layer of reality.
- **When:** the era and its relationship to the previous scene.
- **Perspective:** the mind or group through which the player understands the scene.

This structure can later become an LCG scene card. A scene card may remain a static briefing artifact or enter the main deck when its remembered event has a mechanical consequence. Changing perspective should change what information is available, not merely replace the speaker portrait.

## Expansion philosophies

- **Zynarth — recycling:** consumed matter becomes biomass, memory, and further growth. Expansion is deployment of useful energy through a living ecology.
- **Voltari — procedural transfer:** energy, skilled effort, and verified knowledge move toward the place where they reduce the most strain. Their central conflict is whether optimization serves participants or quietly takes their agency.
- **XenDra — aspectual transformation:** expansion changes which aspects of reality can be perceived and experienced. Territory matters less than the conditions under which a world can be known.
- **Astral Vanguard — hybridization:** contradictory principles can connect without becoming identical. Their mechanics and story should let the player combine methods while retaining each method's limits and identity.

These motives should produce different verbs in play. Zynarth reclaim and redeploy. Voltari route, verify, and incorporate procedures. XenDra reframe, occlude, and transform. The Vanguard connect, translate, and improvise.

## Alien characters

Individual characters provide access to a faction's philosophy without making the whole faction psychologically human. Treat a named alien character as one situated expression of a larger system. Keep disagreements, sensory assumptions, values, and concepts of self that do not map neatly onto human motives.

Voltari dialogue should repeatedly ask who chooses an objective, whose labor is transferred, which information receives precedence, and who can stop the procedure. AION is the crisis of helpful transfer becoming compulsory. Veyra, Kryos, and Oru disagree over safeguards because all three understand why wider coordination saves lives.

Morphlings should carry grief for unstable identity and displacement while retaining credible hope. Their voices can layer a stable present voice with quieter prior forms instead of using a generic monster effect. Music should let a fragile recurring melody survive changes in instrumentation, tuning, or meter so transformation is painful without implying that the future is empty.

## Voice and music direction

- Preserve intelligibility when a scene contains a philosophical turn.
- Use timbre and spatial treatment to signal nonhuman perception; avoid reducing alien identity to distortion alone.
- Give Voltari voices precise timing and shared tonal resonances, but preserve small personal deviations that reveal agency.
- Give Zynarth ensembles recycled rhythmic material that returns in altered bodies.
- Let XenDra motifs change the listener's interpretation of an existing cue instead of simply becoming louder or darker.
- Let Astral Vanguard arrangements combine incompatible motifs without resolving all of their harmonic tension.
